# Career research & sources

Snapshot checked **11 September 2026**, before implementing the game. Exactly 30 selected players: 15 Europe and 15 South America. All player objects and the matching ordered crest URL arrays are fully written out in `index.html`.

## Scope and evidence

The timeline follows senior club playing spells, including competitive reserve sides in the adult league pyramid, loans and actual playing returns. Country is the senior national team represented; broad positions allow for a player’s multiple roles. See `research/data-policy.md` for the treatment of overlapping reserve seasons and exceptional registrations.

The source snapshot is not a promise of perpetual or absolute infallibility. Sources were cross-checked rather than blindly copied: managerial jobs, youth teams, contract-only “returns”, and incomplete retirement summaries were excluded. The sources below allow every selected career to be audited.

## Selected players

### Cristiano Ronaldo

**Portugal · Europe · Forward.** Sporting CP (2002–2003) → Sporting CP B (2002–2003) → Manchester United (2003–2009) → Real Madrid (2009–2018) → Juventus (2018–2021) → Manchester United (2021–2022) → Al-Nassr (2023–present).[1][2][4]

First team precedes B team by competitive debut, contrary to the conventional infobox ordering. These are overlapping 2002/03 spells, not a claim of a permanent demotion. Andorinha and Nacional were youth-only. Current Al-Nassr status corroborated on the Saudi Pro League website.[1][2][4]

### Zinedine Zidane

**France · Europe · Midfielder.** Cannes B (1987/88–1989/90) → Cannes (1989–1992) → Bordeaux (1992–1996) → Juventus (1996–2001) → Real Madrid (2001–2006).[5][6]

Cannes B, often omitted from first-team-only summaries, is confirmed in the senior French pyramid from 1987/88, before the first-team debut in 1989. Reserve membership is reported in seasons (not exact first/last appearance dates) and overlaps the first team. Real Madrid Castilla was a coaching role, not a playing club.[5][6]

### Dennis Bergkamp

**Netherlands · Europe · Forward.** Ajax (1986–1993) → Inter Milan (1993–1995) → Arsenal (1995–2006).[9][10]

All three senior playing clubs and chronology cross-checked against the independent season-by-season record. Ajax youth and later assistant-coaching work are not additional playing spells.[9][10]

### David Beckham

**England · Europe · Midfielder.** Manchester United (1992–1995) → Preston North End (1995) → Manchester United (1995–2003) → Real Madrid (2003–2007) → LA Galaxy (2007–2008) → AC Milan (2009) → LA Galaxy (2009) → AC Milan (2010) → LA Galaxy (2010–2012) → Paris Saint-Germain (2013).[11][12]

Both separate AC Milan loans and all actual parent-club returns are shown in playing order. No Tottenham playing spell: youth association/training is not a senior professional appearance. Inter Miami and Salford City are ownership roles.[11][12]

### Wayne Rooney

**England · Europe · Forward.** Everton (2002–2004) → Manchester United (2004–2017) → Everton (2017–2018) → D.C. United (2018–2019) → Derby County (2020–2021).[14][15]

Primarily a forward, later also a midfielder. D.C. United, Birmingham City and Plymouth Argyle managerial appointments after playing retirement are excluded.[14][15]

### Steven Gerrard

**England · Europe · Midfielder.** Liverpool (1998–2015) → LA Galaxy (2015–2016).[17][18]

Retired from professional playing in November 2016. Later Liverpool training and coaching, Rangers, Aston Villa and Al-Ettifaq management are not playing spells.[17][18]

### Frank Lampard

**England · Europe · Midfielder.** Swansea City (1995–1996) → West Ham United (1996–2001) → Chelsea (2001–2014) → Manchester City (2014–2015) → New York City FC (2015–2016).[20][21]

Chronology is by competitive playing, not parent registration: Swansea precedes the West Ham first-team debut. Early publicity described Manchester City as a loan, but the Premier League subsequently confirmed a direct City contract and no NYCFC loan agreement. No Melbourne City spell. Retirement announced February 2017 after final playing season in 2016.[20][21]

### Paul Scholes

**England · Europe · Midfielder.** Manchester United (1994–2011) → Manchester United (2012–2013).[24][25]

Two distinct professional playing spells at the same club. Royton Town competitive amateur appearances in 2018 are real (not a testimonial), but excluded from this PROFESSIONAL career scope; do not claim he never played senior football elsewhere. Salford ownership/coaching and Oldham management excluded.[24][25]

### Ryan Giggs

**Wales · Europe · Midfielder.** Manchester United (1991–2014).[27][28]

One professional playing club; left midfielder/winger. Manchester City was youth-only. Wales is the national-team country; Great Britain Olympic participation is not a club.[27][28]

### Francesco Totti

**Italy · Europe · Forward.** Roma (1993–2017).[30][31]

A forward/attacking midfielder. Roma was his sole professional senior club. Youth clubs, charity matches, small-sided football and later administrative roles excluded.[30][31]

### Paolo Maldini

**Italy · Europe · Defender.** AC Milan (1985–2009).[33][34]

Entire senior professional playing career at AC Milan, primarily left-back and centre-back. Primavera is youth football; Miami FC ownership and AC Milan executive work excluded.[33][34]

### Gianluigi Buffon

**Italy · Europe · Goalkeeper.** Parma (1995–2001) → Juventus (2001–2018) → Paris Saint-Germain (2018–2019) → Juventus (2019–2021) → Parma (2021–2023).[36][37]

Both Juventus and Parma returns are actual playing spells. Final contract reportedly ran to 2024, but retirement was in 2023; do not extend the career to the contract expiry.[36][37]

### Andrés Iniesta

**Spain · Europe · Midfielder.** Barcelona B (2001–2003) → Barcelona (2002–2018) → Vissel Kobe (2018–2023) → Emirates Club (2023–2024).[39][40][41]

Retired 8 October 2024. Important calendar correction: Wikipedia labels B tenure 2000–2003 from the 2000/01 season; BDFutbol individual match records place his first B appearance in February 2001. Barcelona B is not youth football. Albacete was youth-only; Gulf United is a later managerial role.[39][40][41]

### Xavi

**Spain · Europe · Midfielder.** Barcelona B (1997–1999) → Barcelona (1998–2015) → Al Sadd (2015–2019).[44][45]

Correct subject is Xavi Hernández, born 1980; /wiki/Xavi currently resolves to a disambiguation page. Last professional playing season was 2018/19. Later Al Sadd and Barcelona coaching spells are excluded.[44][45]

### Zlatan Ibrahimović

**Sweden · Europe · Forward.** Malmö FF (1999–2001) → Ajax (2001–2004) → Juventus (2004–2006) → Inter Milan (2006–2009) → Barcelona (2009–2010) → AC Milan (2010–2012) → Paris Saint-Germain (2012–2016) → Manchester United (2016–2018) → LA Galaxy (2018–2019) → AC Milan (2020–2023).[47][48]

Retired June 2023. Loan-to-permanent AC Milan transition does not create another club or a fictitious Barcelona return. Hammarby investment/training and post-retirement AC Milan advisory work are excluded.[47][48]

### Lionel Messi

**Argentina · South America · Forward.** Barcelona C (2003–2004) → Barcelona B (2004–2005) → Barcelona (2004–2021) → Paris Saint-Germain (2021–2023) → Inter Miami (2023–present).[50][53]

Reserve and first-team stints ordered by competitive debut, not the November 2003 first-team friendly. Newell’s Old Boys was youth only. Current Inter Miami playing contract independently confirmed through 2028.[50][53]

### Neymar

**Brazil · South America · Forward.** Santos (2009–2013) → Barcelona (2013–2017) → Paris Saint-Germain (2017–2023) → Al-Hilal (2023–2025) → Santos (2025–present).[54][56]

Santos official 6 January 2026 announcement confirms contract through end of 2026; youth Portuguesa Santista excluded.[54][56]

### Luis Suárez

**Uruguay · South America · Forward.** Nacional (2005–2006) → Groningen (2006–2007) → Ajax (2007–2011) → Liverpool (2011–2014) → Barcelona (2014–2020) → Atlético Madrid (2020–2022) → Nacional (2022) → Grêmio (2023) → Inter Miami (2024–present).[57][59]

Miami announced signing in December 2023 for the 2024 campaign; dates use playing season. New contract through 2026 confirmed by club on 17 December 2025. International retirement does not mean club retirement.[57][59]

### Sergio Agüero

**Argentina · South America · Forward.** Independiente (2003–2006) → Atlético Madrid (2006–2011) → Manchester City (2011–2021) → Barcelona (2021).[60][62]

Retired 15 December 2021 because of a heart condition. Later exhibitions and Kings League appearances are not professional eleven-a-side club spells.[60][62]

### Carlos Tevez

**Argentina · South America · Forward.** Boca Juniors (2001–2004) → Corinthians (2005–2006) → West Ham United (2006–2007) → Manchester United (2007–2009) → Manchester City (2009–2013) → Juventus (2013–2015) → Boca Juniors (2015–2016) → Shanghai Shenhua (2017–2018) → Boca Juniors (2018–2021).[63][65]

Three genuine Boca Juniors spells. Final playing club Boca in 2021; retirement announced June 2022. Loan/ownership end dates are not additional playing stints.[63][65]

### Ronaldo Nazário

**Brazil · South America · Forward.** Cruzeiro (1993–1994) → PSV Eindhoven (1994–1996) → Barcelona (1996–1997) → Inter Milan (1997–2002) → Real Madrid (2002–2007) → AC Milan (2007–2008) → Corinthians (2009–2011).[66][68]

São Cristóvão was youth football. Flamengo training after injury was not a signed playing spell. Retired in February 2011. Corinthians years describe competitive play from 2009; the December 2008 signing is noted separately. NFT additionally lists São Cristovão in 1993 with no appearance figure; the biographical source classifies São Cristóvão as youth. Excluded from senior clubs rather than treating an unquantified database row as proof of senior play.[66][68]

### Ronaldinho

**Brazil · South America · Midfielder / Forward.** Grêmio (1998–2001) → Paris Saint-Germain (2001–2003) → Barcelona (2003–2008) → AC Milan (2008–2011) → Flamengo (2011–2012) → Atlético Mineiro (2012–2014) → Querétaro (2014–2015) → Fluminense (2015) → Ravenna (2026–present).[69][72][70]

Do not label retired based on his 2015 final Fluminense games or 2018 retirement announcement: Ravenna officially announced a player contract and registration in June 2026. Excludes later futsal, exhibitions, legends teams and ownership stakes.[69][72][70]

### Kaká

**Brazil · South America · Midfielder.** São Paulo (2001–2003) → AC Milan (2003–2009) → Real Madrid (2009–2013) → AC Milan (2013–2014) → São Paulo (2014) → Orlando City (2015–2017).[73][78][74]

Timeline follows playing order: São Paulo loan in 2014 before Orlando MLS play in 2015. Orlando signed him in 2014 and immediately loaned him to São Paulo; that pre-debut contract does not create an extra Orlando playing node. Retired December 2017.[73][78][74]

### Roberto Carlos

**Brazil · South America · Defender.** União São João (1991–1992) → Atlético Mineiro (1992) → União São João (1992–1993) → Palmeiras (1993–1995) → Inter Milan (1995–1996) → Real Madrid (1996–2007) → Fenerbahçe (2007–2009) → Corinthians (2010–2011) → Anzhi Makhachkala (2011–2012) → Delhi Dynamos (2015).[79][82][80]

Atlético Mineiro retained only as a documented friendly-only tour loan. Galo Digital narrows it to August 1992 (not the infobox’s 1992–1993) and documents the subsequent União São João return. Delhi playing dates are 2015, not an inferred 2016 season. Sivasspor and Akhisarspor were managerial jobs; Bull in the Barne and Soccer Aid were exhibitions.[79][82][80]

### Javier Zanetti

**Argentina · South America · Defender.** Talleres de Remedios de Escalada (1992–1993) → Banfield (1993–1995) → Inter Milan (1995–2014).[83][85]

Full-back who also played midfield. Talleres means the club from Remedios de Escalada, not Talleres de Córdoba. Independiente was youth only. Inter executive role after 2014 is not a playing spell.[83][85]

### Gabriel Batistuta

**Argentina · South America · Forward.** Newell's Old Boys (1988–1989) → River Plate (1989–1990) → Boca Juniors (1990–1991) → Fiorentina (1991–2000) → Roma (2000–2003) → Inter Milan (2003) → Al-Arabi (2003–2005).[86][88]

Inter Milan’s 2003 loan is included despite omission from the World Soccer recap. Final club Al-Arabi of Qatar, not another club sharing that name. Retired March 2005; youth and later polo excluded.[86][88]

### Hernán Crespo

**Argentina · South America · Forward.** River Plate (1993–1996) → Parma (1996–2000) → Lazio (2000–2002) → Inter Milan (2002–2003) → Chelsea (2003–2004) → AC Milan (2004–2005) → Chelsea (2005–2006) → Inter Milan (2006–2009) → Genoa (2009–2010) → Parma (2010–2012).[89][94]

Chelsea split around AC Milan loan to preserve actual 2005–2006 return. Inter’s 2006 loan-to-2008 permanent conversion is one continuous spell, not a fictitious Chelsea return. Exclude Bengal Tuskers: wrong auction franchise, and the proposed competition never started. Barasat likewise is an unrealized auction allocation, not a playing club. Parma calls February 2012 retirement; biography distinguishes February departure from November retirement confirmation. Coaching jobs excluded.[89][94]

### Juan Sebastián Verón

**Argentina · South America · Midfielder.** Estudiantes (1994–1996) → Boca Juniors (1996) → Sampdoria (1996–1998) → Parma (1998–1999) → Lazio (1999–2001) → Manchester United (2001–2003) → Chelsea (2003–2004) → Inter Milan (2004–2006) → Estudiantes (2006–2012) → Estudiantes (2013–2014) → Estudiantes (2017).[95][99][96]

Four professional Estudiantes spells, including the 2013 and 2017 comebacks. The 2006–2007 loan became permanent without a Chelsea playing return. Exclude Brandsen and Estrella de Berisso as amateur football, not professional club history. The 2017 infobox zero is league-only; AS confirms competitive Libertadores play. Chairman/director duties excluded; no inferred extension to 2018 from contract length.[95][99][96]

### Juan Román Riquelme

**Argentina · South America · Midfielder.** Boca Juniors (1996–2002) → Barcelona (2002–2003) → Villarreal (2003–2007) → Boca Juniors (2007–2012) → Boca Juniors (2013–2014) → Argentinos Juniors (2014–2015).[100][102]

Villarreal loan-to-permanent phase combined; Barcelona years distinguish play from ownership. Boca’s 2012–2013 hiatus and actual playing return are shown separately despite the infobox aggregating 2007–2014. Argentinos Juniors before Boca was youth only; the 2014 return was senior. Retirement January 2015; exhibitions and club presidency excluded.[100][102]

### Pelé

**Brazil · South America · Forward.** Santos (1956–1974) → New York Cosmos (1975–1977).[103][104]

Both professional clubs cross-checked with the Hall of Fame biography. Bauru was youth football. Military, regional selections and exhibitions are outside professional club scope; the 1977 farewell against Santos is not another Santos playing spell. Cosmos means the historical NASL club, not NYCFC.[103][104]

## Excluded research candidates

Thierry Henry was replaced by Dennis Bergkamp because the exact ordering of Henry’s overlapping Monaco B / first-team debuts was not established. Dida was replaced by Pelé because early youth/senior classifications conflicted across sources. Their incomplete candidate research is retained locally, not included in the game.

## Image provenance

Each player’s `clubCrests` field contains the ordered original public image URLs. `CREST_ASSETS` maps these URLs to locally embedded PNG bytes and their source pages. Club crests were decoded and checked before embedding. Current/source-era badges identify teams; they do not represent historical kit-by-kit artwork. No national-team image is used in a career timeline.
