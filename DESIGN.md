# derabona — design & acceptance contract

## Product
A football-career guessing game with offline guest play in one portable `index.html`: no frontend build, framework or external fonts. Optional Google accounts use Supabase Auth, private PostgreSQL state and Edge Functions for persistent ranked play; public browser configuration is not a server credential. Consent-first PostHog analytics remains optional and separate. These are implemented contracts, not a claim of hosted deployment or OAuth verification. See [accounts and leaderboards](docs/leaderboards.md) and [ADR 0006](docs/adr/0006-accounts-and-ranked-progress.md). 110 curated players (and growing with each research batch — see the footer of the app itself for the current total), split equally between European and South American national teams, organized into five competitions (Champions League, Premier League, La Liga, Argentine Primera División, Brasileirão) plus an All Players mode. Data verification precedes app implementation; supporting research lives alongside (not required to play).

## Visual direction
**derabona / fútbol, de memoria.** An Argentinian football-memory identity with an original rabona-player badge and a heavy italic lowercase wordmark. Warm match-programme paper around an ink-blue pitch; celeste accents, compact records-style labels and authentic club crests. See [BRAND.md](BRAND.md) for logo sources, usage and tokens.

- Paper `#f7f4eb`; ink `#122a38`; celeste `#89cff0`; deep-blue text accent `#176180`; panel `#fffdf7`; muted text `#52636a`.
- Success `#17613e` on pale green; error `#a93637` on pale red. Text/icons accompany both colors.
- System sans-serif body, heavy editorial headings and monospaced metadata. No runtime font downloads.
- Clubs are grouped into pages of up to eight (a 4x2 block) in the DOM (ADR 0018). At 800px and below, pages are invisible to layout (`display:contents`), so every club flows into one continuous four-column grid that grows to as many rows as needed, with no horizontal scrolling (ADR 0016). Above 800px, each page becomes its own real two-row grid and a career longer than one page adds another page alongside it, scrolling sideways instead of growing a third row, so the answer options below stay on-screen (ADR 0017/0018). Reading order is plain row-major at every width — left to right, then down — the same as mobile always used; a two-attempt column-major ("zigzag") design was tried and rejected as confusing even after correcting its connector direction once. The small numbered badge over each crest stays hidden always; it exists only so automated tests can verify DOM order, since the reading order already makes chronology obvious without it.
- On mobile, the competition selector and score sit in their own header row below the logo/language row. Keep the longest career's crests inside the initial 375×667 viewport and avoid horizontal scrolling.
- The masthead (`<header class="masthead">`) holds only the brand and a top-right `.masthead-right` (language selector + the account control) - the same two-element shape as `leaderboard.html`'s masthead, with matching padding, so the header bar and its bottom border sit at very nearly the same height on both pages and the border doesn't visibly jump when navigating Play ↔ Leaderboards. The competition badge and the single Play↔Leaderboards nav action share one row below the masthead's border (`.page-nav-row`: the badge, then a `.page-nav-actions` group holding the nav link and `#play-mode`) rather than two stacked rows - same height, less vertical space, and page-specific content still never moves back into `<header>`. The badge leads with an icon instead of a dropdown-style caret: a trophy (`#competition-badge-trophy`) for "All Players", swapped for the selected competition's own crest (`#competition-current-logo`) once one is chosen - `setCompetitionLogo()` toggles between them, so the badge always signals "this opens a competition picker" rather than reading as a plain `<select>`. It carries no visible "Competition"/"Competición" label (that's `sr-only`), the icon and clickable pill styling do that work instead. Toggling the trophy's visibility needs `toggleAttribute('hidden', …)`, not `.hidden = …` - SVGElement doesn't reliably reflect the `hidden` IDL property to the content attribute the way HTMLElement (the crest `<img>` beside it) does, so a plain property assignment can leave `[hidden]{display:none!important}` never matching while `el.hidden` still reads back `true`. The competition picker lists one competition per row (not a 2-column grid) so a long name (Premier League, Champions League, Argentine Primera División) never wraps to two lines. "How to play" lives in the footer, next to the scoring explanation. The hero's short tagline paragraph and the career panel's redundant bottom bar (a "SENIOR CLUB CAREER" label plus a spell-count/swipe-hint caption, duplicating what's already visible from the crests and the region's own scroll `aria-label`) were removed as pure noise; the pitch's decorative center-circle mark was removed too since it visually collided with the crest discs on several career lengths, and its decorative outline border is hidden entirely on the mobile numbered grid (`@media(max-width:800px)`) since a career can wrap to any number of rows there and a fixed-inset outline cuts across whichever row it lands on instead of framing the content.
- The game has exactly one headline, and it's the masthead tagline - there's no separate large hero `<h1>` any more. `#title` moved onto the tagline itself (`<h1 id="title">` under the wordmark, was `<small>`; visually unchanged, font-weight explicitly reset to 400 since `<h1>` defaults to bold where `<small>` didn't), and the old hero section (`Adiviná al jugador. Por su carrera.` / `Know the clubs. Name the player.`, plus all of `.hero`/`.hero h1`'s now-dead CSS across every breakpoint) is gone entirely - the game panel now starts almost immediately after the masthead. This is a deliberate escalation of the "game is the focus, not chrome" direction: first the redundant eyebrow slogan went, now the redundant *headline* goes too, leaving one real heading doing both jobs.
- That heading is the brand's one slogan, directly under the wordmark in the masthead (`.brand h1` on the game, `#wordmark-tagline`/`<small>` on the leaderboard, which keeps its own separate, correct page `<h1>` - "Clasificación"/"Leaderboard" - so its tagline stays a `<small>`), always visible on both pages including mobile (the old `display:none` mobile override is gone - there's nothing left above it to make redundant on a small screen): "Adiviná al jugador. De memoria. De rabona." / "Guess the player. From memory. With a rabona." - a pun on the brand name itself (see BRAND.md), styled with the same two-tone treatment the old hero headline used (first clause muted, the rest in the accent color via a `<span>`). Both copy keys (`brand`/`tagline`) carry that `<span>` as real markup, so their DOM update is `innerHTML=` (`$('title')` on the game, right alongside the other headline-style copy `applyLanguage()` already updates that way; a dedicated line on the leaderboard), not the generic `textContent=` label loop each page otherwise uses (that loop would print the `<span>` tags as literal text). (None of this touches `.summary-panel .eyebrow`/the leaderboard's own "PUNTOS VERIFICADOS" eyebrow, which are page-local section labels, not app-level headline/tagline content.) `.brand` on the game and `.wordmark` on the leaderboard are both real links to `index.html` - clicking the logo always returns to Play, the universal convention.
- Play ↔ Leaderboards is one contextual action, not a two-tab pair: each page's nav shows only a link to the *other* page, since there's no second tab left to contrast against and a link to the page you're already on doesn't tell you anything. `aria-current="page"` is gone along with the self-link it described. Both links are filled pills in the logo's own ink color (not the earlier outlined/transparent style) with a leading icon instead of an arrow: a trophy (same mark as `#competition-badge-trophy`, reused rather than redrawn) for "Clasificación", a simple filled play-triangle for "Jugar" - the icon says what pressing the button leads to, the color says it's a first-class action tied to the app's own identity. The label text lives in its own child span (`#leaderboard-open-label` / `#play-link-label`) so the translation update (`textContent=`) doesn't wipe out the icon alongside it.
- The competition picker's "All Players"/"Todos los jugadores" row now has an icon too, like every other row: the real app icon (`BRAND_MARK_ICON`, the masthead `.mark` SVG re-serialized as a data: URI *with* its square background, unlike the rect-stripped `BRAND_MARK_SKIN` used for the pitch watermark) rather than a real competition crest, since "all players" has no crest of its own.
- Keep the compact `#play-mode` pill hidden for the ordinary guest and ranked states; it remains reserved for practice/loading/unavailable transitions. Guest ranking consequences instead use the deliberately visible `#guest-ranked-status` directly above the career on configured online signed-out play. That strip is consequential system-state copy, not generic chrome: it states tab-local score, no leaderboard inclusion and no transfer after sign-in, and its CTA opens Account without blocking play. It is omitted from portable `file:` play. On narrow mobile it wraps into a compact two-row layout without horizontal overflow; the career remains immediately below it ([ADR 0020](docs/adr/0020-guest-ranked-disclosure.md)).
- On the mobile numbered grid, each club renders inside its own subtle bordered card (`.club{background:...;border:...}` at `@media(max-width:800px)`), not just floating text - a loan/reserve/return tag on some clubs but not others means rows don't share a uniform height, so a border per club (not just more row gap) is what actually keeps a revealed "years" line unambiguously paired with the crest above it rather than possibly reading as belonging to the row below.
- `#speed-meter`, a thin line along the very top edge of the career panel, gives a deliberately minimal live readout of the speed-decay curve (`CareerGame.timeFactor`, `docs/adr/0009-speed-decay-anti-lookup-tightening.md`) for the active round, in guest/practice and ranked alike. Its fill is a rescale of `timeFactor`'s 0..1 output (`speedMeterFraction` in `game-ui`) that maps the score's own floor (currently 25%, never lower) to a *fully empty* bar rather than one resting partway full: a bar that visibly stopped draining once time was "up" read as stuck or broken, even though the underlying score genuinely never drops to zero for a win - the bar is a simplified time-pressure gauge, not a literal points meter, and the click-to-reveal note still states the real (never-zero) points. It freezes the instant the round resolves (a class swap, not a stopped animation - the button's hit area is intentionally taller than the visible line for a workable tap target without growing the line itself). Clicking it any time reveals `#speed-meter-note`: elapsed seconds while playing, or - once resolved - the actual hint count and points earned (0 for a loss). It reads ranked's server-persisted `started_at` directly rather than keeping its own timer, so it can never drift from what the server will actually charge.
- The career panel (`.pitch`) carries a deliberately faint per-competition "skin": the selected competition's own crest (from `COMPETITION_LOGOS`), or - for All Players, which has no crest of its own - the brand mark itself, rendered as a soft off-corner silhouette (`#pitch-skin`, a `background-image` layer behind the z-index:1 content, `grayscale+brightness(0)+invert` turning any source art into a flat white shape regardless of its original colors, at ~4.5% opacity). It's confined to this one panel - the page's own paper background never changes - and is tuned to read as texture, not as a legible logo/wordmark on sight: something noticed on a second look, not a branding moment. `setCompetitionLogo(id)` (in `game-ui`) is the single point that swaps it, alongside the existing competition-badge logo, so it updates on load, language switch and every competition change (guest and ranked) without a separate call site to keep in sync.
- Points are shown in the masthead only once signed in - an anonymous guest total has no leaderboard to compare against, so it stays hidden rather than displaying a number with no audience. Guest wins call the local value "guest score" / "puntos de invitado" and "unranked" / "sin clasificación". When cloud points are visible, the score is merged directly into the account control itself (icon + points + label, e.g. "🧑 1,234 Account") rather than sitting in an adjacent button. Signed out, the control visibly says "Sign in to compete" / "Iniciar sesión para competir" above 580px; narrow phones collapse it to an icon with the same accessible name. Guests keep session streak/history reachable via `#stats-detail-open` in the recap panel.
- The account dialog leads with one bold, brief sentence explaining the actual payoff of signing in (compete for points against players worldwide) before the longer storage/privacy paragraph, and its Google button follows Google's own sign-in button convention (white surface, the real multi-color "G" mark, Google's button proportions/typography) instead of the site's lime primary-action style - "Sign in with Google" is a pattern people already recognize on sight, so it shouldn't be reskinned to look like a generic app button.
- The account dialog is grouped into clear sections rather than one flat list of differently-sized buttons: pitch/status copy, then primary actions (a full-width Google button when signed out; "Cerrar sesión"/"Volver a clasificación" as evenly-sized pills when signed in), then a bordered nickname/alias section (label and info-tooltip icon on one row, the input stacked full-width below, "Guardar apodo" as the one clear primary action), then a separately bordered danger zone holding only "Eliminar cuenta" styled as a de-emphasized red underlined text action rather than a same-weight button, then "Cerrar" bottom-right matching every other dialog's close-button placement. The alias info tooltip is a real `position:absolute` popover anchored to its own icon+tooltip wrapper, not an unstyled paragraph pushing the layout around when shown. The wrapping `#alias-section`/`#danger-section` containers are hidden (not just their inner controls) while signed out, so there's no stray empty divider line. Once signed in, the generic "here's how guest progress vs. an account works" paragraph (`#account-detail`) - genuinely useful before signing in, dead weight once you already have - is replaced by three real numbers instead (`#account-stats`: points, matches played, correct guesses), the same data and wording ("Jugadas"/"Played", not "Carreras"/"Careers" - avoids reading as "races" in Spanish, decontextualized from the rest of the app's "career" language) as the leaderboard table's own columns.
- `#account-open` on the game uses a visible benefit-specific signed-out label above 580px, then an icon plus accessible name on narrow phones. Once signed in, its existing compact icon + point-total/account treatment remains. `#account-open-link` on the leaderboard keeps that page's established compact account treatment; shared dimensions, focus styling and breakpoint alignment still need cross-page verification even though the signed-out label visibility now intentionally differs on the game.
- Answer targets remain at least 58px on narrow mobile and 65px otherwise. Preserve keyboard focus, readable supporting text, localized live feedback and reduced-motion support.
- The logo and favicon are embedded in `index.html`. Preserve legacy storage namespaces and all gameplay during the rebrand.

## Leaderboard destination
- Rankings live at a directly addressable `leaderboard.html`, not an overlay. Primary Play / Leaderboard navigation uses real links and indicates the current page. Keep Account as a separate dialog on the game.
- Preserve the editorial paper/navy/celeste identity. Prioritize board scope, personal position, then rank/name/points. Keep secondary counts readable without horizontal scrolling on short mobile screens.
- Global and competition filters share the existing immutable-result views; URL state preserves language/scope and browser navigation. Never invent weekly seasons, rank deltas or podium tie-breaks.
- Show authenticated personal rank even outside the visible page; distinguish guests, no verified result, empty board, loading and retryable errors. Include a clear Play action.
- The page is read-only: no automatic round start, gameplay save writes or guest import. Auth/SDK failures must not block anonymous boards. Clear stale personal state on account switches. No analytics on this page.
- Dedicated static artifact stays lightweight; keep the offline guest game portable. See [ADR 0008](docs/adr/0008-standalone-leaderboard-page.md).
- Navigating Play ↔ Leaderboard must not feel like landing on a different app: `leaderboard.html`'s embedded CSS mirrors `index.html`'s shared chrome values deliberately (not just similar) - the same `width:min(1120px,calc(100% - 64px))` container formula (and its narrower-breakpoint reductions), the same masthead padding/border, the same wordmark/mark sizing and lockup (logo, italic "derabona", and the same muted mono tagline underneath it - hidden on narrow phones on both pages, not present on one and absent on the other), a matching top-right `.masthead-right` (language selector + an icon+label "Sign in"/account control, collapsing to icon-only on narrow phones exactly like `index.html`'s), and the same eyebrow (mono, uppercase, muted, rotated-square accent-color bullet) and `h1` (`font-weight:850`, matching `clamp()`/letter-spacing at every breakpoint) treatment. `leaderboard.html`'s eyebrow is its own page-specific label ("Verified points"/"Puntos verificados"), distinct from the shared brand tagline under the logo - reusing the same string for both reads as a stutter when they sit one above the other. When either page's shared-chrome CSS changes, check the other for drift - re-verify with real measurements (bounding boxes of `header.masthead`, `h1`, `nav`, `.brand`/`.wordmark`), not just a glance or a partial checklist, since a few pixels or one missing line is exactly what reads as "everything moved" when a user clicks between the two.

## Game contract
- Choose a competition (Champions League, Premier League, La Liga, Argentine Primera División, Brasileirão) or All Players (the default) from the prominent competition badge, reachable at any time and never forced. The chosen competition persists in the saved game state and survives reload. The badge and the picker's option list both show the competition's real logo (`COMPETITION_LOGOS`, see BRAND.md) next to its name; "All Players" has no logo. Picking a competition never scrolls the page — it's chosen from the masthead, already at the top.
- Tapping the masthead score badge opens a dialog with the current/best win streak and this session's results history, instead of a permanently visible streak indicator taking up masthead space.
- Guest decks shuffle only unseen players in the selected competition without replacement; totals and recap boundaries derive from the saved deck, not current roster length. Replay preserves accumulated points and excludes resolved players, reopening the competition picker when exhausted. Accounts use one server-created active round at a time, shared across devices; requesting another competition resumes that active round instead of abandoning it.
- A resolved player — right or wrong — is excluded across competitions: within the guest tab session until unranked Reset, or permanently per account/player/ruleset for ranked play. Exhausted competitions are **Completed**, disabled, and never provide ranked repeats. ADR 0006 supersedes the device-lifetime/reset scope of [ADR 0005](docs/adr/0005-permanent-seen-ledger-and-completed-competitions.md). Its documented legacy guest boot fallback is not permission to add a ranked repeat.
- Each player record explicitly contains country, broad pitch position, ordered club metadata, crest image URLs, a pool of incorrect player names, and one or more competition tags.
- Every round samples nine distinct distractors from the current player's pool (a deliberate harder-game decision - up from the original four), inserts exactly one correct name, then shuffles all ten. Ranked mode's server schema now deals the same nine distractors/ten total, under a new `v2` ruleset (ADR 0012) - the original `v1` five-option results remain immutable, just excluded from the active board. Exclude any player with an identical ordered club-name signature from the distractor pool so the visible career cannot have two valid choices. The competition determines the answer deck, not a hard rival filter. Starting domestic football system is a strict rival tier before broader region; only widen when necessary to obtain the required number of names, preserving all tighter-tier matches.
- Use origin-first Hard automatically, with no difficulty selector. Starting origin comes from the first displayed senior club, not nationality or youth career. Within the required origin tier, rank by `6 × shared clubs + 12 × same opening club + 4 × same national team + 8 × overlapping broad role + 8 × career-year intersection/union − 2 × debut-year difference − 0.5 × ending-year difference + 2/(1 + club-count difference)`, with a 2-point same-competition bonus. Reserve B/C sides share their parent for classification/scoring, not chronology or ambiguity exclusions. See [ADR 0002](docs/adr/0002-origin-first-distractors.md).
- Supplement playable players with the independently researched wrong-answer-only bank; never expand playable decks implicitly. Within each origin tier, prefer overlapping careers with debut differences at most eight years. Retain all tighter-tier matches if fewer than four; in the boundary tier rank by similarity plus uniform noise in `[0,3)`, taking exactly the remaining slots. Never drop a candidate for one more than three score points worse in the same tier. Shuffle final positions. See [ADR 0003](docs/adr/0003-researched-contemporary-rivals.md).
- Normalize old saved difficulty preferences to Hard on load. Repair outdated origin/era choices only in the current round with zero guesses and zero hints; preserve already-compatible options and all engaged rounds exactly, including answer order. Deck, points, competition and history are unchanged. Future rounds, competition changes, reset and replay use Hard. Keep historical results and their localized difficulty labels intact; the level helpers remain internal for legacy testing.
- Three attempts per round. A wrong answer turns red, disables itself, and consumes one attempt only. A correct answer turns green, animates, awards points and increments consecutive wins. A loss reveals the correct answer and resets the streak.
- Scoring (see `docs/adr/0001-local-results-history-and-speed-based-scoring.md`, retuned by `docs/adr/0009-speed-decay-anti-lookup-tightening.md` and again by `docs/adr/0011-longer-decay-window-and-guest-clock-persistence.md`): a round is worth up to 100 points, reduced 20% per hint used (a hard cap regardless of speed) and by answer speed — full value inside a 2s grace window from when the round became active, decaying to a 25% floor by 24s. A won round always scores at least 10. Guest elapsed time persists per active round (`sessionStorage`, keyed to the current player), same as ranked's server `started_at`, so reload cannot zero the clock. For a brand-new canonical-site round with no saved clock, the guest timer waits while the first analytics-consent choice obscures play, then starts and persists immediately after either decision; an existing saved clock is never reset ([ADR 0020](docs/adr/0020-guest-ranked-disclosure.md)).
- Guest score/streak and the recap total accumulate across decks in the current tab session, tallied once at resolution. Competition switches and replay do not reset them; unranked Reset does. The recap supporting line describes its saved deck. Ranked mode displays authoritative global account points in the masthead and currently a dash for streak; never mix cloud and guest totals. ADR 0006 supersedes the device-lifetime scope of [ADR 0004](docs/adr/0004-lifetime-score-persists-across-decks.md).
- Finished guest games enter a tab-session history capped at 100 games. Confirmed Reset clears only unranked current game, history, score/streak and seen ledger; it is disabled in account mode. Ranked results have no reset/import/replay endpoint. Explicit account deletion is separate from Reset and removes the account and its cloud/public records.
- Hints reveal country, then position, then each club's years in the career timeline, in that exact order — capped at 3, identically in guest/practice and ranked (the former fourth hint, initials, was removed: combined with the visible name options it made the answer too obvious). Years are hidden by default in the timeline to reduce visual noise and only shown once the third hint is spent, at the same cost as the other two. All revealed hints remain visible; repeated clicks after the third do nothing. Ranked mode is server-authoritative (see `docs/adr/0010-ranked-third-hint-parity.md`); the server only needs to authorize the hint count, not send a years value, since the client already has the full career locally from the same embedded roster guest mode uses.
- Finish a round before Next Player appears. All options and hints lock on completion. Rapid repeated clicks cannot award extra points or skip multiple rounds.
- Display current streak, round progress and attempts left; show masthead points only for an authenticated ranked account. Guest totals remain in session results, while terminal feedback explicitly labels awarded local value as guest score and unranked. Round progress ("X OF Y COMPLETE") counts every resolved player in the *whole competition* (guest session ledger or cloud results), not just this specific unseen sub-deck — switching away and back must never show a lower completed count than before. The guest round-number label ("03 / 52") stays deck-local; ranked mode labels the server round as ranked. Save guest state in sessionStorage when available, with memory fallback; restricted storage must not prevent unranked play.
- Source links and career caveats become available after answer reveal, never leak the answer before play.
- English/Spanish UI, hints, notes, rules and accessibility labels share one offline artifact. Language changes never reset game state. Explicit URL language overrides saved preference, which overrides the Spanish default. Preserve original proper names and source article titles.

## Account, storage and ranking contract

- Guest gameplay keys retain their legacy `touchline.*.v1` names but use `sessionStorage` per tab. Refresh normally retains progress; normal close ends the session, with browser session restore caveats. Denied storage falls back to memory; reload then restarts. Language and analytics permission/identity remain independent in localStorage. Supabase auth persistence uses `derabona.auth.v1`.
- Legacy localStorage gameplay migrates only as a linked unranked snapshot. Stage and read back all keys before removing any originals. Never fill missing session keys from a conflicting legacy snapshot; keep originals and offer explicit replacement recovery. Preserve engaged rounds and history. No local score or seen ledger becomes a ranked result.
- Sign-in is optional. Before gameplay, configured online signed-out play visibly states that guest score is tab-local, unranked and never transferred; after the first resolved guest result, one inline reminder reinforces the boundary. Both sign-in CTAs open Account and preserve the existing engaged-guest confirmation before Google OAuth; cancellation preserves the session. Sign-out starts fresh guest play. Account results appear automatically under a stable, unique random animal alias after a verified result. No manual public-enrollment step is required. While that automatic alias remains unchanged, Account may prefill the optional nickname field with the Google profile name as an editable suggestion; it is never submitted or published unless the player explicitly saves it, and Google photo/email are never published ([ADR 0019](docs/adr/0019-suggest-google-name-as-nickname.md)). An accessible EN/ES tooltip explains that the player can optionally add a custom nickname in Account. Nicknames are 3–24 permitted letters/numbers/spaces/underscore/dot/hyphen, trimmed and case-insensitively unique.
- Only accounts with at least one result in the selected board appear; account creation automatically supplies an alias and participation. A loss is a result even at zero points. Global points sum each first verified player result once; competition boards filter canonical player memberships, irrespective of the deck where answered. They are overlapping views, not additional score awards. Ties share rank; deterministic nickname ordering is pagination, not a tiebreak.
- The server owns ranked options, hints, attempts, time and points. Guest time uses a per-player session clock and includes reload/time away once started; the first consent overlay may delay only a brand-new unsaved guest clock. Ranked time uses the persisted server start, so reload/offline time never restarts that window. The client submits only allowed actions with server versions and idempotency keys, never points, elapsed time or identity.
- Cloud/auth/network failures lock ranked actions and show Retry or explicit unranked practice. Practice is never queued for upload or silently upgraded after reconnect. Retry uncertain mutations with the same key/body, then reconcile authoritative progress; explicitly rejected payloads must be correctable. Exhausted starts return a completed state with no round, not a revived previous result.
- Authentication and account deletion are functional service requests, not analytics. Analytics consent is neither required for accounts nor granted by Google login or public enrollment. Never identify account users in PostHog or send nicknames, account IDs, Google data, OAuth callback values or tokens. The public bilingual `privacy.html` belongs in the static package.
- Private service-only state, immutable first results and transactional locking protect score integrity, not secrecy of the public offline roster. No claim of bot-proof play or prevention of multiple Google accounts.

## Data contract
- Research the full senior career and country/position using retrieved career records plus independent corroboration. Date the snapshot; do not imply active-player data remains current forever.
- National teams, youth teams, coaching teams and testimonials never appear as timeline clubs. The documented Roberto Carlos senior tour loan is the explicit friendly-only exception and is visibly tagged.
- Include senior reserve-team spells and actual loans/returns. Reserve/first-team dates can overlap; document ordering. Do not invent parent playing spells between back-to-back loans.
- Distinguish announced signings from completed registration and appearances. Career-stint endpoints may denote departure or retirement rather than a last match; disclose this explicitly and never extend a retired career to an unfulfilled contract expiry. See DATA_AUDIT.md.
- Preserve continuous loan-to-permanent spells as one node, annotated. Preserve genuinely separate return spells as separate nodes.
- Embed fetched crest bytes as data URLs for reliable offline rendering. Keep original public image URLs for provenance. Crest trademarks remain their owners'; no club affiliation implied. Do not pretend a fallback is an authentic crest.

## Verification matrix
1. Data: exactly 110 unique player IDs/names; continent count 55/55; sources for every entry; all ordered club names mapped to valid crest bytes; no national-team nodes; every pool has four or more distinct incorrect names excluding the answer; every player carries at least one recognized competition tag.
2. Options: exactly ten unique buttons (guest/practice Hard) with one correct answer across every player and many shuffles, both unscoped and within each competition; ranked now also deals ten, under the new `v2` ruleset (ADR 0012).
3. Correct first/second/third try: score increments once, streak increments once, next appears, controls lock.
4. Wrong choices: disable/red/attempt decrements, repeated selection no-op, third error ends round, correct answer revealed, streak resets.
5. Hint order and boundary: guest/practice and ranked country → position → club years, cap three; hints do not consume attempts but reduce awarded points.
6. Deck/progress: guest saved unseen decks finish at their own length; replay/competition changes preserve cumulative points and seen players. Ranked first results never repeat across competitions/devices; exhausted starts return completed/null and do not revive an old round. Rapid clicks cannot duplicate points.
7. Persistence: reload recovers attempts, hints, choices, score, deck and the active competition; published 30/40/50-player full-roster saves finish their original decks under a missing (implicitly "all") competition field; malformed storage falls back safely; denied storage works.
8. Browser: no console errors; embedded images decode; file/HTTP guest play works offline without external requests. Account SDK/API failures leave explicit unranked practice usable and never credit offline answers. PostHog stays silent without consent, including around OAuth callbacks.
9. Responsive/accessibility: every player in both languages at 320, 375, 430, 580, 768 and 1440px; every club visible in the numbered grid without horizontal overflow at any of those widths. Thumb-sized answers, keyboard focus, translated live feedback, reduced motion, language persistence, and a complete Spanish playthrough are required.
